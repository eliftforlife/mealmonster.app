What 1,000 Weight Loss Transformations Have in Common

By Eli, founder of Meal Monster · former personal trainer with a nutrition certification · Updated 2026-10-19

The short answer

  • The most common combination: one way of eating plus one way of moving. 48% of stories did both.
  • The single most common eating change was tracking food / counting calories (25%), more than keto (14%) or fasting (21%).
  • The median story used 3 methods, not 10.
  • Only 7% used GLP-1 medication or surgery. 93% used neither.
  • People who had failed before were 1.7× more likely to use a fasting window and 1.7× more likely to meal prep the time it worked.

How we got the data

We took the top 1,000 weight loss story videos on YouTube by views (18 first-person searches like "how I lost weight" and "my weight loss journey", English, 2 to 90 minutes, no minors), downloaded the captions (296 hours of video, 3.2 million words), and counted a method only when the person said they did it themselves, in their own video. Stories YouTube shows the most are the dramatic ones, so this skews toward big results. It describes what people did, not what caused their results.

The top of the ladder isn't one method. 72% moved their body, 60% changed how they eat, and 48% did both, the biggest group of all. 24% only exercised and 12% only changed their eating.

The results they reported

762 stories said how much they lost. The median was 50 lb (middle half: 30 to 100 lb), and 30% lost over 100 lb. Of the 390 stories that gave a timeframe, the median was 3 months. These are the stories YouTube promotes, so if yours is slower, that's normal.

What the people who had failed before did differently

165 stories (16.5%) said they had tried before and failed. The time it worked, they were 1.7× more likely to use a fasting window (33% vs 19%) and 1.7× more likely to meal prep (10% vs 6%) than everyone else. Fewer decisions in the moment, more decided ahead of time. This is who chose what, not proof of what caused success.

What they ate

The most mentioned foods were chicken (102 stories), vegetables (88), rice (72), salads (71) and eggs (60). But chocolate (47), ice cream (45) and pizza (44) showed up in dozens of stories too. Plenty of them still ate the foods they loved.

What nobody puts in the thumbnail

36% talked about cravings, 17% about slipping up, and 10% had lost weight before and gained it back. If that's you, you're in very normal company.

How to copy the top of the ladder

  1. Pick one way of eating: smaller portions, a fasting window, or tracking what you eat.
  2. Pick one way of moving: walking or lifting.
  3. Decide it the night before.
  4. Tell one person.
  5. Give it 3 months, expect cravings, and don't quit over one bad day.

Questions

What do most successful weight loss stories have in common?

In 1,000 top YouTube weight loss stories, the most common pattern was one change to eating plus one way of moving: 48% did both. The median story used 3 methods.

What is the most common way people lost weight?

Among eating changes, tracking food or counting calories was the most common (25% of stories). Among exercise, lifting weights (49%) and cardio (47%) were the most common.

How many people used Ozempic or surgery?

4% mentioned GLP-1 medication and 3% weight loss surgery. 93% of the stories used neither.

How long did it take?

Of the 390 stories that gave a timeframe, the median was 3 months, but these are the most-viewed stories, which skew dramatic. Slower progress is common.

Is this medical advice?

No. It's an analysis of what people said they did in public videos. Talk to a qualified professional before big changes.
