What 447 Skinny-to-Muscular Transformations Have in Common

By Eli, founder of Meal Monster · former personal trainer with a nutrition certification · Updated 2026-10-19

The short answer

  • 65% changed how they eat, more than the 51% who lifted. The most common pair, 38%, did both: they ate more and they lifted.
  • The median story used just 2 methods.
  • Extras were rare: creatine came up in 6% of stories, a coach in 11%.
  • The median person gained 22 lb, and the median timeframe was 3 months (the middle half took 1 to 12).
  • The #1 struggle was appetite (19%), then the cost of food (14%).

How we got the data

We took the top 1,000 "how I gained weight" and skinny-to-muscular videos on YouTube by views (20 first-person searches). 324 had no usable speech (montages and music), we left out 155 minors or school-age creators (adults only), and removed 26 that were about losing weight or cutting. That left 447 adult first-person stories (94 hours). A method counts only when the person says they did it themselves. It describes what people did, not what caused their results.

The top of the ladder isn't the gym on its own. 65% changed how they eat, 51% lifted (including progressive overload), and 38% did both.

The results they reported

231 stories said how much they gained. The median was 22 lb (middle half: 20 to 30 lb). Of the 146 that gave a timeframe, the median was 3 months, but the middle half took anywhere from 1 month to a year. If yours is slower, that's normal.

What made eating more easier

People who got full fast ate more often instead of bigger (17%), drank calories with shakes (21%), and leaned on foods that pack a lot into a little, like peanut butter, oats and rice (27%).

What nobody shows

38% talked about staying consistent or missing meals, 19% struggled with appetite, 14% with the cost of food, and 8% gained more fat than they wanted. Protein mattered, but one creator called eating "150 to 200 g of protein" with barely any carbs a mistake.

"There was no structure, no plan."

How to copy the top of the ladder

  1. Know your reason. It's what gets you to eat on the days you're not hungry.
  2. Add one meal or snack a day.
  3. Track what you eat for one week, so you know it's actually enough.
  4. If you get full fast, use shakes and foods that are easy to eat a lot of.
  5. Lift on a program you'll stick to.
  6. Give it 3 months.

Questions

How do skinny people gain weight and muscle?

In 447 adult first-person stories, 65% changed how they eat and 51% lifted weights. The most common combination, 38% of stories, was eating more plus lifting. The median story used 2 methods.

Do you need creatine or a mass gainer to gain weight?

Most people in the data didn't use them: creatine came up in 6% of stories and shakes or mass gainers in 21%. Eating more and lifting were far more common.

How much weight did people gain, and how fast?

Of the stories that said, the median gain was 22 lb and the median timeframe was 3 months, with the middle half taking 1 to 12 months.

What if I get full too fast?

Appetite was the most common struggle (19%). People ate more often instead of bigger, drank shakes, and chose foods that pack a lot of calories into a small amount.

Is this medical advice?

No. It's an analysis of what adults said they did in public videos. Talk to a qualified professional before big changes, especially if you're underweight for a medical reason.
