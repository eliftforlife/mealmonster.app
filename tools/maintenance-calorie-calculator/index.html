How the maintenance number is worked out

Step 1: your resting burn (BMR). We use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the formula many dietitians start with:

  • Men: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
  • Women: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

Step 2: your activity. BMR times an activity factor gives your maintenance calories, also called TDEE:

  • Mostly sitting: 1.2
  • Light (on your feet, or 1 to 3 workouts a week): 1.375
  • Moderate (3 to 5 workouts a week): 1.55
  • Very active (6 to 7 hard workouts a week): 1.725

For example, a 35-year-old woman who is 5 ft 5 in (165 cm) and 150 lb (68 kg) has a BMR of about 1,375. With light activity (× 1.375) her maintenance is about 1,890 calories a day.

Step 3: check it against real life. Formulas can be off by a few hundred calories either way. Eat close to the number for 2 to 3 weeks and watch your weight trend. If it holds steady, that is your maintenance. If it drifts, adjust by 100 to 200 calories.

Questions

What are maintenance calories?

The calories you can eat in a day and keep your weight about the same. It is the same number as your TDEE, total daily energy expenditure.

How do I find my maintenance calories?

Start with a calculator estimate like this one, then eat close to it for 2 to 3 weeks. If your weight holds steady, that is your maintenance. If it drifts up or down, adjust by 100 to 200 calories.

Is maintenance the same as TDEE?

Yes. TDEE is the calories you burn in a day, and eating about that much keeps your weight about the same.

Why is my maintenance different from 2,000 calories?

The 2,000 on nutrition labels is a general reference for comparing foods, not a personal number. Your size, age, sex and activity change it.

How accurate is a maintenance calorie calculator?

It is an estimate. Studies of the Mifflin-St Jeor equation find it lands within about 10% for most adults, and some people are further off. Your weight trend over a few weeks is the better guide.

Who is this calculator for?

Adults 18 and over. Teens are still growing, so their needs are best set with a doctor or dietitian. It also is not built for pregnancy or nursing.
