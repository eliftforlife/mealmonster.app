      What does this look like?

      Dry pasta

      Barilla describes its dry pasta serving as uncooked. If you use that serving’s numbers, pair them with uncooked pasta—not the heavier pasta after boiling. Check your own product’s label. Barilla’s guidance ↗

      Canned tuna

      StarKist’s 5-ounce Chunk Light Tuna in Water label gives a drained serving. That weight describes the tuna after draining, rather than the full can with liquid. Package sizes differ, so use your own label. See the maker’s label ↗

      Frozen or ready-cooked food

      Frozen does not tell you whether food is raw or cooked. “As packaged” means the food in the state you bought it. Follow the package wording and directions; do not choose a raw entry just because it was frozen.

      Sources and limits

      A guide to U.S. label wording for adults 18+. Labels, recipes and package sizes vary. We do not guess a cooking conversion, identify an unlabeled food, or replace instructions from its maker. If your package is from another country, verify its wording with the maker.

      • U.S. food-label rules: food as purchased, drained solids and prepared columns.
      • USDA Foundation Foods documentation: edible portions and material not eaten.
      • Manufacturer examples: Barilla and StarKist. Examples apply to the described products, rather than every pasta or can.
